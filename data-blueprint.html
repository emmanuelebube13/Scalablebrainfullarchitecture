Data Blueprint

Two views of the same system. The conceptual model says what the business things are and how they relate, with no tables and no technology. The data flow diagrams say where data enters, which program moves it and where it rests today.

Conceptual modelThe things the business cares about and the relationships between them. It is the target every store is measured against.
Data flow, contextThe system as one box, and everything outside it that sends or receives data.
Data flow, level 1The inside of the box: programs, stores and the data moving between them, as it runs today.

Conceptual model

Read the heavy row first: a signal is judged by a risk decision, which approves an order; the order is filled, and fills update a trade. Everything else either feeds that row or describes it.

Data flow, context

One PC holds everything. The practice broker account is the only place orders go. Two outputs are meant to exist and do not work yet, and two kinds of account are not connected.

Data flow, level 1, as it runs today

Signals from two producers meet on one queue. Guardian, the bridge and Hand pass orders and fills over that same queue, and only Hand talks to the broker. Everything below the trade path is copying and watching.

Questions for the reviewer

  1. Are these the right 24 business things, and is anything missing or split wrongly?
  2. Should a signal belong to a strategy version, so that every trade can be traced to exact code and parameters?
  3. Should account and environment be plain attributes, so that a live or prop-firm account is one more row?
  4. Which store should own each thing? The trade and the strategy are the two with the most copies today.
  5. Should the dashboard read the owning store directly and show the age of every copy it relies on?
